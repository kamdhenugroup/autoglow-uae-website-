Every paint protection film installer in Dubai will tell you they use the best film. Most of them are telling the truth, because the film is the easy part.

Thermoplastic polyurethane from a reputable manufacturer performs to specification regardless of who buys the roll. What varies enormously, and what you are actually paying for, is whether the person holding the squeegee knows how to tuck an edge, whether your car spends eight hours in a dust free bay or in an open workshop off 22nd Street, and whether anyone registers your warranty afterwards.

These nine questions will tell you which kind of shop you are standing in. We have included what a good answer sounds like and what a bad one sounds like, because the phrasing matters more than the yes or no.

Fair warning on bias: we install PPF. Read this as a checklist to use on us as well.

## 1. Are you an authorised dealer for this film, and will you register the warranty in my name?

This is the first question because it is the one that most often exposes a problem.

Manufacturer warranties are conditional on certified installation. XPEL's limited warranty on Ultimate Plus and Stealth runs ten years from the date of initial installation and covers both the film and the labour to remove and replace it, and it transfers to a subsequent owner. But that coverage only exists if an authorised dealer registers your vehicle in the manufacturer's system after the job. You should receive a warranty confirmation with your registration details on it.

If nobody registers the install, you have a car with film on it and no warranty behind it. The film may still be genuine. Your recourse if it yellows in year three is nothing.

**Good answer:** "Yes, we are an authorised dealer. We register through the manufacturer portal on completion and you will get the confirmation by email with your VIN on it. I can show you a sample certificate now."

**Bad answer:** "Yes, of course, ten year warranty, no problem." No mention of registration, no document offered.

Also worth knowing: XPEL does not run a tiered dealer programme. Installers are either authorised dealers or they are not. So if a shop is advertising itself as a platinum, elite, or diamond level partner, that badge was made in a design tool, not issued by the manufacturer.

## 2. Which exact film, and which product line?

"XPEL" is a brand, not a product. "Premium PPF" is not a product at all.

Ask for the specific product name and the thickness in mils. Premium self healing films sit around 8 mil. Ask whether you are being quoted the flagship gloss film, the matte or satin variant, or a lower tier line from the same manufacturer at a lower price point.

Equally, the market has more than one legitimate premium film. XPEL, SunTek, and 3M all produce genuine self healing TPU films with long warranties. A shop that tells you every film other than the one it stocks is rubbish is selling, not advising.

What you are looking to avoid is the substitution: quoted on a flagship film, invoiced on it, and installed with something cheaper. Which brings us to the receipt.

**Ask for the film brand and product line to be written on the quotation and the final invoice.** A shop with nothing to hide will do this without hesitation.

## 3. Computer cut from the pattern database, or hand cut on my paint?

Premium installers work from digital patterns. XPEL's DAP library holds somewhere around 80,000 to 90,000 vehicle specific kits, and it exists precisely so that nobody has to run a blade across your clear coat.

Hand cutting on the vehicle is not automatically wrong. An experienced installer will hand trim selectively to get a better edge on a complex panel, and the result can be superior to a plotted pattern. The problem is a shop that hand cuts everything because it does not have pattern access, using a blade on your paint, on a car that may already have thin clear coat from a previous polish.

**Good answer:** "Computer cut from the pattern database for the panels, and we hand trim only at these specific points because it produces a cleaner wrap there."

**Bad answer:** "We cut by hand, it is better." Followed by no explanation of where or why.

If your vehicle is rare enough that no pattern exists, a competent shop will tell you that upfront and explain how they will bulk cut off the vehicle instead.

## 4. Do you disassemble, or do you cut around?

This is the question that separates a good looking result at delivery from a good looking result in year two.

Proper installation means removing badges, lights, mirrors, door handles, and trim so film can be wrapped around and tucked behind edges. Film that is tucked has no exposed edge to lift, collect dust, or peel. Film that is cut flush to a panel edge has a visible line, and in this climate that line becomes a dust trap and then a lifting point.

Ask specifically: which components come off, and are the edges wrapped or relief cut? Ask what happens at the bonnet leading edge, the door jambs, and the wheel arch lips.

**Good answer:** A specific list of what comes off, and an honest note about anything they will not remove and why.

**Bad answer:** "We wrap everything, no cutting." Nobody wraps everything. That answer means they have not thought about it.

## 5. Where does my car actually sit for the next two days?

In Dubai this is not a minor logistical question. It is a quality question.

PPF is installed wet, using slip solution, and it needs to be squeegeed, tacked, and left to release moisture. Airborne dust in the bay ends up under the film permanently. Ambient temperature affects how the adhesive tacks and how the film stretches. An open workshop in August, with the roller door up and a shamal blowing through, is not a viable environment for this work no matter how skilled the installer is.

Ask to see the installation bay. Not the reception, not the showroom. The bay.

You are looking for: enclosed, filtered or positive pressure air, temperature control, proper lighting overhead and at low angles, and a clean floor. You are also looking for whether other work is happening in the same space, because sanding or bodywork nearby puts particulate into the air your film is going onto.

Ask where the vehicle sits overnight and during the post installation cure period. Outdoors, in a car park, in direct sun, is a different answer from indoors.

## 6. Will you measure and record my paint depth first, and can I see the readings?

Any preparation before film goes on may involve polishing. Polishing removes clear coat. Clear coat is finite.

A serious shop puts a paint depth gauge on every panel before touching the car, records the readings, and shows them to you. This matters enormously on pre owned vehicles in the UAE market, where a car may have been resprayed or heavily polished by a previous owner or dealer, and where the remaining clear coat may not tolerate correction at all.

It also protects you. Documented pre existing readings and photographs mean any dispute later has evidence attached to it.

**Good answer:** They show you the gauge, explain what the numbers mean, and flag any panel reading unusually high or low as a likely respray.

**Bad answer:** "The paint looks fine."

If the car needs correction before film, that is a separate line on the quote and should be described in stages. Film locks in whatever defects are underneath it.

## 7. What exactly does this package cover, panel by panel, in writing?

"Full front" means different things in different workshops. So does "front end", "partial front", and "high impact package".

Get the coverage written out. Specifically:

- Bonnet: full, or partial? If partial, how far back, and is there a visible line across the bonnet in daylight?
- Front bumper: complete, including the lower valance and the areas behind the front wheels?
- Fenders: full, or just the leading section?
- Mirrors: caps only, or the full housing?
- Headlights and fog lights: included?
- A pillars and roof leading edge: included?
- Door cups, door edges, rocker panels, rear arch impact zones?
- Door jambs where film wraps into the shut line?

Two quotes that look thousands of dirhams apart often turn out to be quoting different jobs. Once both are written out panel by panel, the comparison becomes honest.

## 8. What is your own workmanship warranty, and who pays if an edge lifts in year two?

The manufacturer warranty and the installer warranty are different things, and shops routinely blur them.

The manufacturer covers defects in the film: yellowing, cracking, bubbling, peeling, delamination. It does not cover a poorly tucked edge that lifted because the installer rushed it. That is workmanship, and it is on the shop.

So ask directly: how long is your workmanship warranty, what does it cover, and if an edge lifts in eighteen months, do I pay for anything?

Also ask what conditions attach to the manufacturer warranty on the specific product you are buying. Some coverage carries maintenance obligations, and certain XPEL warranties require a documented annual inspection by a certified installer within a defined window around the anniversary date, with failure to complete it voiding the coverage. Read your own certificate rather than relying on what you were told at the counter.

**Good answer:** A stated period, in writing, with the exclusions named.

**Bad answer:** "Lifetime warranty." Nothing on a car is lifetime.

## 9. Can I see a car you did more than a year ago, in daylight?

Anyone can produce a good photograph of a freshly installed car in a lightbox at delivery. Film hides nothing at that stage.

What you want to see is a vehicle that has lived through a UAE summer. Look at the edges for lifting and dust intrusion. Look at the bonnet leading edge and the wheel arches for staining. Look at the film over a light colour for yellowing. Ask the owner, if you can, what the shop did when something needed attention.

If a shop cannot produce a single older car or an owner willing to speak, that is information.

## Red flags

- A quote given over WhatsApp with no inspection of the vehicle.
- Pressure to pay a deposit today because the price expires today.
- Refusal to name the specific film product on the invoice.
- The installation bay is not shown to you.
- Manufacturer warranty and workmanship warranty described as the same thing.
- "Lifetime" anything.
- A price dramatically below every other quote. The film has a fixed material cost. The saving is coming out of preparation, disassembly, bay conditions, or the pattern.
- Badges claiming dealer tiers that the manufacturer does not issue.
- No written record of pre existing paint condition.

## Questions that sound smart but do not tell you much

To be even handed, some commonly repeated advice is close to useless:

- **"How many years have you been in business?"** Longevity says nothing about the individual installing your car this week. Ask about the installer, not the trade licence date.
- **"Do you use ceramic infused film?"** The hydrophobic top coat is a modest convenience feature. It is not a reason to choose one shop over another.
- **"Is it self healing?"** Every premium film is. This filters nothing.
- **"How many cars have you done?"** Volume can indicate speed rather than care.

The questions that discriminate are the ones about process, environment, documentation, and accountability. Those are hard to fake in conversation.

## The short version

If you only ask three, ask these: **will you register the warranty in my name and show me the certificate, can I see the installation bay, and will you write the coverage out panel by panel?**

A shop that answers all three cleanly is very unlikely to be cutting corners elsewhere.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much should PPF cost in Dubai?**
Pricing depends on vehicle size, coverage package, film product line, and whether paint correction is required first. The material cost of premium film is broadly fixed across the market, so unusually low quotes generally reflect reduced preparation, less disassembly, or a lower tier film rather than a better deal.

**Does PPF have to be installed by an authorised dealer?**
For the manufacturer warranty to be valid, yes. XPEL requires certified installation and registration of the vehicle for warranty claims to be honoured.

**Is computer cut PPF better than hand cut?**
Computer cut patterns remove the need to run a blade on your paint and give repeatable fitment. Skilled hand trimming can produce a better edge on complex panels. The best installers use pattern cutting as the base and hand trim selectively, and will tell you exactly where.

**How long does a PPF installation take?**
A full front package is typically a full day, and full body coverage runs across several days including disassembly, preparation, installation, and cure time. A shop offering full body in a single day is compressing something.

**Can PPF be removed later?**
Yes. Quality film is designed to be removed without damaging factory paint, which is one reason it is favoured on vehicles where resale value matters.

**Does the PPF warranty transfer if I sell the car?**
On XPEL Ultimate Plus and Stealth, the limited warranty transfers to a subsequent owner, which is why the registration record matters.

**What happens if my installer closes down?**
Manufacturer coverage sits with the manufacturer, not the shop, provided the installation was registered. Workmanship coverage does not survive the shop. This is the practical reason to prioritise registration.
